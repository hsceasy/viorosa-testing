Quadratic graphs and parabolas

Algebraic Relationships course notes

Key concept

A quadratic graph is a parabola. A positive coefficient of x² opens upwards; a negative coefficient opens downwards. The turning point is a minimum or maximum.

y = ax² + bx + c, where a ≠ 0

Worked example 1

Identify the turning point and intercepts of y = x² − 4.

The minimum is (0, −4). Set y = 0: x² = 4, so x = ±2. The y-intercept is (0, −4).

Worked example 2

Describe y = −x² + 9.

Opens downwards; maximum (0, 9); x-intercepts (−3, 0) and (3, 0).

Common mistake

A quadratic may have zero, one or two distinct x-intercepts.

Axis of symmetry from intercepts

Consider y = −x² + 8x. For x = 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 the outputs are 0, 12, 16, 12, 0. The x-intercepts are 0 and 8; their midpoint is 4, so the axis is x = 4. The vertex is (4,16). Enter y=-x^2+8x in a graphing application and check the intercepts and maximum.
