Reciprocal graphs and asymptotes

Algebraic Relationships course notes

Key concept

A reciprocal relationship has the form y = k/x with k nonzero. The graph is a hyperbola with two separate branches.

y = kx, x ≠ 0

Worked example 1

Describe y = 4x.

Vertical asymptote x = 0; horizontal asymptote y = 0. No axis intercepts. The branches lie in quadrants I and III.

Worked example 2

If y = 12x and x doubles from 3 to 6, what happens to y?

y changes from 4 to 2: it halves.

Common mistake

Never connect the two branches across x = 0.

Inverse variation in context

Six workers complete a job in 10 hours. Assuming equal constant productivity, worker-hours k = 6 × 10 = 60. Thus t = 60/n. For 3, 6, 10 and 15 workers the times are 20, 10, 6 and 4 hours. To finish in 5 hours, solve 5n=60, so n=12. The graph can also be read at t=5.

Only positive whole numbers of workers are meaningful. The curve is shown from 3 workers to fit the scale; the model cannot include zero workers and ignores coordination overhead.

Now you try: 8 workers take 9 hours. Time for 12?

72 ÷ 12 = 6 hours.
