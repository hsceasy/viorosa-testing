Quadratic models and maximum values

Algebraic Relationships course notes

Key concept

Quadratic models can describe height, area or revenue. The turning point gives the maximum or minimum within the model domain.

For y = a(x − h)² + k, the turning point is (h, k).

Worked example 1

A ball has height H = −(t − 3)² + 9 metres, for 0 ≤ t ≤ 6. Find its maximum height.

The turning point is (3, 9): maximum height 9 m after 3 s.

Worked example 2

When does the ball return to ground level?

Set H = 0: (t − 3)² = 9, giving t = 0 or 6. It returns after 6 s.

Common mistake

Only use times within the physical domain.

Interpret a practical parabola

A ball has height H = −t² + 8t metres. The graph shows launch at 0 s and landing at 8 s. The midpoint of the intercepts is 4 s; maximum height is 16 m. At 2 s and 6 s the height is 12 m. The physical domain is 0 ≤ t ≤ 8 and range 0 ≤ H ≤ 16; negative predicted heights are not valid after landing.

Now you try: a ball lands after 10 s and starts at ground level. When is its maximum?

5 s, assuming a symmetric quadratic flight model.
