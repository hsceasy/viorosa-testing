Credit cards and borrowing costs

Investment and Loans course notes

Credit options

Compare total repayments, interest, establishment fees, late fees and term. Buy now, pay later may have no interest but can charge fees. A short-term loan may have a high cost relative to its term; long-term loans can accumulate more interest.

A $600 purchase with four $150 payments and a $10 fee costs $610. A loan with six $110 payments costs $660.

Paying only $25 leaves $592 before new interest or transactions. An interest-free period is conditional on the card terms, often requiring full payment by the due date. Cash advances may attract immediate interest and fees.

Daily compounding example: $800 at 18% p.a. for 30 days gives interest $800[(1 + 0.18 ÷ 365)30 − 1] = $11.92. Actual card terms may use a different calculation.

Compare credit cards for short-term purchases, personal loans for medium-term goals and longer-term secured loans for major purchases, considering total cost and affordability.

Worked example: minimum repayments

Compare a personal loan at 12% nominal annual interest: on $2,000, first-month interest is $20 rather than $40. With equal $200 monthly payments and no fees, the card costs $2,000.57 interest on a $5,000 balance versus $782.45 for a personal loan on the same $5,000 balance. Compare fees and terms as well as rates. Real cards often calculate interest daily.
