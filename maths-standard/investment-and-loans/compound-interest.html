Present value, rate and compounding frequency

FV = PV(1 + r)n. To find present value, divide FV by (1 + r)n. To find r, take the nth root of FV ÷ PV and subtract 1.

Example: $1,331 after 3 years from $1,000 implies r = 1.3311/3 − 1 = 10% p.a.

To reach $1,210 in two years at 10%, invest $1,000 now. At 6% p.a. compounded monthly, r = 0.005 and n = 12 per year. $2,000 becomes $2,123.36 after one year.

Inflation and appreciation use the same growth model: a $500 item rising 4% annually costs $540.80 after two years.

Higher rates, longer terms and more frequent compounding (at the same positive nominal annual rate) increase future value.

Compare compounding periods

Invest $5,000 for three years at 6% nominal annual interest. Divide the annual rate by the number of compounding periods per year, and multiply the term by that number.
