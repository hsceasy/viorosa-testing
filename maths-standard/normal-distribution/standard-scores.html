Calculating z-scores

The Normal Distribution course notes

Key concepts

A z-score is the signed distance from the mean in standard deviations. Standardised normal data have mean 0 and standard deviation 1. Positive scores are above the mean; negative scores below.

z=x−μσ (x = observation, μ = mean, σ = standard deviation); x = μ + zσ

Worked example

A test has mean 70 and SD 8. Mia scores 86. Find and interpret her z-score.
Worked solution
  1. z = (86−70) ÷ 8 = 2.
  2. Mia scored two standard deviations above the mean.

Now you try

A machine fills bottles with mean 500 mL and SD 5 mL. A bottle contains 490 mL. Find z and the volume corresponding to z=1.
Show answer
  1. z = (490−500) ÷ 5 = −2.
  2. x = 500 + 1×5 = 505 mL.
