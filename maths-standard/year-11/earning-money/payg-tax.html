PAYG tax

Key idea

Pay-As-You-Go (PAYG) withholding is money withheld from pay during the year and credited against final tax liability. If withholding exceeds the liability, the difference is a refund; otherwise the difference is owed.

Annual withholding

Weekly withholding × 52

Refund

PAYG withheld − final tax and levy (if positive)

Amount owing

Final tax and levy − PAYG withheld (if positive)

Use this Australian resident income tax table for 2024–25 and 2025–26 classroom examples. Rates exclude Medicare levy. Ignore offsets and other adjustments.

Taxable income

Assessable income − allowable deductions

Taxable income × 0.02

Assume the full 2% levy applies; real reductions and exemptions may apply.

Tax refund

A worker earns $64,000, claims $1,500 in deductions and has $230 withheld weekly. Find (a) taxable income, (b) bracket, (c) tax and levy, (d) refund or debt.

(a) Taxable income = 64,000 − 1,500

= $62,500

(b) $45,001–$135,000 bracket.

(c) Tax = 4,288 + 0.30(62,500 − 45,000)

= 4,288 + 0.30 × 17,500

= 4,288 + 5,250

= $9,538

Levy = 0.02 × 62,500

= $1,250

Total liability = 9,538 + 1,250

= $10,788

(d) PAYG = 230 × 52

= $11,960

Refund = 11,960 − 10,788

= $1,172

Net annual earnings after final tax and levy = 64,000 − 10,788

= $53,212
