Inflation and vehicle costs

Key idea

Inflation is a general rise in prices. Vehicle costs include purchase price, stamp duty, transfer fees, registration, compulsory third-party (CTP) personal injury insurance, optional property or comprehensive insurance, loan repayments, fuel and maintenance. CTP does not cover damage to vehicles. Actual charges vary; use the supplied rates.

Inflation

New price = original price × (1 + inflation rate as a decimal)

Fuel used

distance (km) × fuel consumption (L per 100 km)100

Total loan repayments

Repayment amount × number of repayments

Worked example 1

A $250 weekly grocery bill increases by 4% then 3%. Find the new bill.

Year 1 = $250 × 1.04 = $260

Year 2 = $260 × 1.03 = $267.80

Worked example 2 — buying a car

A car costs $18,000. Assume stamp duty is 3%, transfer fee $40, registration $350 and CTP $450. Find the total upfront cost.

Duty = $18,000 × 0.03 = $540

Total = $18,000 + $540 + $40 + $350 + $450

Total = $19,380

Worked example 3 — finance and running costs

A driver borrows $15,000 and repays $350 monthly for 48 months. Optional insurance costs $900 annually, maintenance $600 annually and the car travels 12,000 km annually at 7 L/100 km. Fuel costs $1.90/L. Annual registration and CTP together cost $800. Find total loan repayments and annual running costs including repayments.

Loan repayments = $350 × 48 = $16,800

Finance cost above principal = $16,800 − $15,000 = $1,800

Annual repayments = $350 × 12 = $4,200

Fuel used = 12,000 × 7 ÷ 100 = 840 L

Fuel cost = 840 × $1.90 = $1,596

Annual total = $4,200 + $900 + $600 + $1,596 + $800

Annual total = $8,096
