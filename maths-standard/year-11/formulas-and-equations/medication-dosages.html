Medication dosage formulas

Key idea

Historical classroom models only, never use for actual medication dosing. Let A be the adult dose and D the modelled child dose. Fried’s: D = mA150 (m in months). Young’s: D = aAa+12 (a in years). Clark’s: D = wA150 (w in pounds; use the units specified in the question).

Fried’s formula: calculate D

For hypothetical A = 100 units, m = 15.

D = 15 × 100150

D = 1500150

D = 10 units

Young’s formula: solve for age

For hypothetical A = 120 units, D = 40 units.

40 = 120aa+12

40(a+12) = 120a

40a+480 = 120a

480 = 80a

a = 6 years

Clark’s formula: solve for adult dose

For hypothetical w = 50 lb, D = 20 units.

20 = 50A150

3000 = 50A

A = 60 units

Check units, brackets and each inverse operation before finalising your answer.
