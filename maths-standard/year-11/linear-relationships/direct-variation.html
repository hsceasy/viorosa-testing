Direct variation and squared relationships

Direct variation

y = kx

k is the constant of variation. The graph passes through the origin.

Four steps

  1. Choose pronumerals and set up y = kx.
  2. Substitute known values and solve for k.
  3. Rewrite the formula with k.
  4. Substitute the new value and solve for the unknown.

Worked example 1 — cost

A printer charges in direct proportion to the number of posters. Six posters cost $18. Find the cost of 14 posters.

Solution

Let C = cost ($), n = number of posters.

1. C = kn

2. 18 = 6k

186 = 6k6

k = 3

3. C = 3n

4. C = 3(14)

C = 42

14 posters cost $42.

Worked example 2 — distance

A cyclist travels at a constant speed. In 2 hours she travels 36 km. How long will 63 km take?

Solution

Let d = distance (km), t = time (h).

1. d = kt

2. 36 = 2k

362 = 2k2

k = 18

3. d = 18t

4. 63 = 18t

6318 = 18t18

t = 3.5

3.5 hours (3 hours 30 minutes).

Squared relationships

y = kx²

y varies directly with the square of x, not with x itself. Doubling x multiplies y by four.

Worked example 3 — square variation

The area A of a square display varies directly with the square of its side length s. A display with side 3 m has area 9 m². Find the side length for an area of 25 m².

Solution

A = ks²

9 = k(3)²

9 = 9k

99 = 9k9

k = 1

A = s²

25 = s²

√25 = s (s ≥ 0)

s = 5

The side length is 5 m.
