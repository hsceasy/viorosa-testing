Linear models and intersections

Linear model

y = mx + c

The vertical intercept c is the starting value; gradient m is the rate of change.

Worked example 1 — delivery

A courier charges $8 plus $2 per kilometre. Find the cost of a 12 km delivery.

Solution

C = 2d + 8

C = 2(12) + 8

C = 24 + 8

C = 32

The cost is $32.

Worked example 2 — comparing plans

Plan A charges $10 plus $3 per visit. Plan B charges $22 plus $1 per visit. When do they cost the same?

Solution

A = 3v + 10

B = v + 22

3v + 10 = v + 22

3v − v + 10 = v − v + 22

2v + 10 = 22

2v + 10 − 10 = 22 − 10

2v = 12

2v2 = 122

v = 6

A = 3(6) + 10

A = 18 + 10

A = 28

B = 6 + 22 = 28

Both plans cost $28 at six visits; intersection (6, 28).

Limitations

A model simplifies reality. A courier may charge extra for long distances or change rates. Visits must be whole numbers and cannot be negative. A straight-line prediction is only reasonable while its assumptions hold.
