VIOROSA MATHS · YEAR 11 · MATHEMATICS STANDARD

Dijkstra shortest path algorithm

Viorosa Maths · Year 11 Mathematics Standard

Key idea

For nonnegative weights, repeatedly settle the smallest tentative distance and update neighbouring distances.

Worked example 1

AB = 2, AC = 6, BC = 1: settle B at 2 then C at 3.

Worked example 2

AB = 5, AC = 2, CB = 1: settle C at 2 then B at 3.

Common mistake: Settling a vertex before its tentative distance is smallest.

Step-by-step: six junctions

Start with distance 0 at A and infinity elsewhere. At each step choose the unsettled vertex with the smallest tentative distance. For each unsettled neighbour, add the edge weight to the settled distance; replace its label only if the result is smaller. Record the predecessor when replacing a label. This works for nonnegative weights.

Step 1: settle A at 0

B: 0 + 4 = 4 replaces ∞; C: 0 + 2 = 2 replaces ∞

Distance labels: A: 0, B: 4, C: 2, D: ∞, E: ∞, F: ∞

Step 2: settle C at 2

B: 2 + 1 = 3 replaces 4; E: 2 + 7 = 9 replaces ∞

Distance labels: A: 0, B: 3, C: 2, D: ∞, E: 9, F: ∞

Step 3: settle B at 3

D: 3 + 5 = 8 replaces ∞

Distance labels: A: 0, B: 3, C: 2, D: 8, E: 9, F: ∞

Step 4: settle D at 8

E: 8 + 2 = 10 (keep 9); F: 8 + 6 = 14 replaces ∞

Distance labels: A: 0, B: 3, C: 2, D: 8, E: 9, F: 14

Step 5: settle E at 9

F: 9 + 3 = 12 replaces 14

Distance labels: A: 0, B: 3, C: 2, D: 8, E: 9, F: 12

Step 6: settle F at 12

No unsettled neighbours remain.

Distance labels: A: 0, B: 3, C: 2, D: 8, E: 9, F: 12

Read the route backwards

F came from E, E from C and C from A. Reverse this chain: A–C–E–F. Total 12 minutes. The rose edges in the step diagrams show current predecessor links, not necessarily the final route.
