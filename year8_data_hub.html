Lesson 01

Mean

Add all values and divide by the number of observations.

Worked example

Find mean of 2, 4, 6, 8.

Mean = 2+4+6+84
= 204
= 5

Now you try

Find mean of 3, 5, 7, 9.

Show worked solution

Mean = 3+5+7+94
= 244
= 6

Lesson 02

Median

Order the values; average the two middle values for an even number.

Worked example

Find median of 8, 2, 4, 6.

Ordered: 2, 4, 6, 8
Median = 4+62
= 102
= 5

Now you try

Find median of 9, 3, 7, 5.

Show worked solution

Ordered: 3, 5, 7, 9
Median = 5+72
= 6

Lesson 03

Mode and range

Mode is the most frequent value; range is maximum minus minimum.

Worked example

Find mode and range of 2, 3, 3, 8.

Mode = 3 (twice)
Range = 8 − 2
= 6

Now you try

Find range of 4, 5, 5, 10.

Show worked solution

Range = 10 − 4
= 6

Lesson 04

Frequency tables

Multiply each value by its frequency to calculate the total.

Worked example

Values 1, 2, 3 occur 2, 3, 1 times. Find mean.

Total = 1 × 2 + 2 × 3 + 3 × 1
= 2 + 6 + 3
= 11
Count = 2 + 3 + 1 = 6
Mean = 116

Now you try

Values 2, 4 occur 3, 1 times. Find mean.

Show worked solution

Total = 2 × 3 + 4 × 1
= 10
Count = 3 + 1 = 4
Mean = 104
= 2.5

Lesson 05

Dot plots

Each dot represents one observation. Compare centre and spread on the same scale.

Worked example

A: 2, 3, 3, 4; B: 1, 3, 3, 5. Compare ranges.

A range = 4 − 2 = 2
B range = 5 − 1 = 4
B has greater range.

Now you try

Which set has greater range: A: 2, 4, 6; B: 3, 4, 5?

Show worked solution

A range = 6 − 2 = 4
B range = 5 − 3 = 2
A

Lesson 06

Stem-and-leaf plots

The key explains place value; order leaves within each stem.

Worked example

Key 2
3 = 23. Leaves for stem 2 are 1, 4, 8. Find range.

Values: 21, 24, 28
Range = 28 − 21
= 7

Now you try

Key 3:2 = 32. Leaves for stem 3 are 2, 5, 9. Find median.

Show worked solution

Values: 32, 35, 39
Middle value = 35

Lesson 07

Histograms

Adjacent bars represent numerical class intervals. For equal-width intervals, height represents frequency.

Worked example

Intervals 0–<10, 10–<20, 20–<30 have frequencies 2, 5, 3. Find total.

Total = 2 + 5 + 3
= 10

Now you try

Frequencies 4, 6, 2. Find total.

Show worked solution

Total = 4 + 6 + 2
= 12

Lesson 08

Distribution shape

A positively skewed distribution has a longer right tail; a negatively skewed one has a longer left tail.

Worked example

Most values are small, with a few much larger values. Identify shape.

Tail extends to larger values.
Positively skewed.

Now you try

Most values are large with a few very small values. Identify shape.

Show worked solution

Tail extends left.
Negatively skewed.

Lesson 09

Outliers

An unusually distant value may affect mean and range more than median.

Worked example

Compare means of 2, 3, 4 and 2, 3, 4, 19.

Original mean = 93 = 3
New mean = 284 = 7
Increase = 7 − 3 = 4

Now you try

Find mean of 1, 2, 3, 14.

Show worked solution

Mean = 1+2+3+144
= 204
= 5

Lesson 10

Choosing measures

Median is often more representative when extreme values distort the mean.

Worked example

Choose a centre for incomes 30, 32, 35, 200 thousand dollars.

Mean = 2974 = 74.25
Median = 32+352 = 33.5
Median better represents typical income.

Now you try

Which centre is less affected by a single extreme value: mean or median?

Show worked solution

Median uses ordered middle values.
Median

Lesson 11

Sampling and bias

A sample should represent the population. A census collects data from everyone.

Worked example

A school surveys only basketball players about favourite sport. Explain.

Basketball players are overrepresented.
The sample may be biased.

Now you try

Which is more representative: random students or only basketball players?

Show worked solution

Random students from across the school.

Lesson 12

Comparing distributions

Compare centre, spread, shape and unusual values in context.

Worked example

A: 4, 5, 6; B: 1, 5, 9. Compare.

Both medians = 5
A range = 6 − 4 = 2
B range = 9 − 1 = 8
B is more spread out.

Now you try

A range 3, B range 7. Which is more spread out by range?

Show worked solution

7 > 3
B
