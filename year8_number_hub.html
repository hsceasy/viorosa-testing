Lesson 01

Integer direction and magnitude

Absolute value is distance from zero.

Worked example

Find the absolute value of −8.

Distance from −8 to 0 = 8
Absolute value = 8

Now you try

Find the absolute value of −12.

Show worked solution

Distance from −12 to 0 = 12

Lesson 02

Adding integers

Adding a negative moves left on the number line.

Worked example

Calculate 5 + (−9).

5 + (−9) = 5 − 9
= −4

Now you try

Calculate 7 + (−11).

Show worked solution

7 + (−11) = 7 − 11
= −4

Lesson 03

Subtracting integers

Subtracting a negative is adding its opposite.

Worked example

Calculate −3 − (−8).

−3 − (−8) = −3 + 8
= 5

Now you try

Calculate −4 − (−10).

Show worked solution

−4 + 10
= 6

Lesson 04

Multiplying integers

Same signs give positive products; different signs give negative products.

Worked example

Calculate (−4) × (−6).

4 × 6 = 24
Same signs → +24

Now you try

Calculate (−7) × 3.

Show worked solution

7 × 3 = 21
Different signs → −21

Lesson 05

Dividing integers

Apply the sign rule to the quotient.

Worked example

Calculate −48 ÷ 6.

48 ÷ 6 = 8
Different signs → −8

Now you try

Calculate −63 ÷ (−9).

Show worked solution

63 ÷ 9 = 7
Same signs → +7

Lesson 06

Order of operations

Brackets, powers, multiplication and division left to right, then addition and subtraction left to right.

Worked example

Calculate 8 − 3 × (−2).

= 8 − (−6)
= 8 + 6
= 14

Now you try

Calculate 12 − 4 × (−3).

Show worked solution

= 12 − (−12)
= 12 + 12
= 24

Lesson 07

Divisibility

A number is divisible by 3 if its digit sum is divisible by 3.

Worked example

Is 372 divisible by 3?

3 + 7 + 2 = 12
12 ÷ 3 = 4
Yes.

Now you try

Is 435 divisible by 3?

Show worked solution

4 + 3 + 5 = 12
12 ÷ 3 = 4
Yes.

Lesson 08

Common factors

The highest common factor is the greatest factor shared by both numbers.

Worked example

Find HCF of 18 and 24.

Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18
Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24
Common: 1, 2, 3, 6
HCF = 6

Now you try

Find HCF of 12 and 20.

Show worked solution

Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
Factors of 20: 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20
HCF = 4

Lesson 09

Rational numbers

A rational number can be written as a fraction of integers with nonzero denominator.

Worked example

Express −0.75 as a fraction.

−0.75 = −75100
= −34

Now you try

Express 0.6 as a fraction in simplest form.

Show worked solution

0.6 = 610
= 35

Lesson 10

Ordering rational numbers

Convert to a common representation before comparing.

Worked example

Which is smaller: −0.6 or −34?

−34 = −0.75
−0.75 is left of −0.6
Therefore −0.75 is smaller.

Now you try

Which is smaller: −0.2 or −0.5?

Show worked solution

−0.5 lies to the left of −0.2
−0.5 is smaller.

Lesson 11

Fraction operations

Use common denominators for addition; multiply by reciprocal for division.

Worked example

Calculate −23 + 14.

= −812 + 312
= −512

Now you try

Calculate −34 ÷ 25.

Show worked solution

= −34 × 52
= −158

Lesson 12

Recurring decimals and checking

Recurring decimals are rational. Use exact fractions to check calculator approximations.

Worked example

Convert 0.222… to a fraction.

x = 0.222…
10x = 2.222…
10x − x = 2.222… − 0.222…
9x = 2
x = 29

Now you try

Convert 0.444… to a fraction.

Show worked solution

x = 0.444…
10x = 4.444…
10x − x = 4
9x = 4
x = 49
