Mean

The mean is a measure of centre: add all values and divide by the number of values. It represents an equal share.

Worked example

Find the mean of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10.

Mean = 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + 105
= 305
= 6

Now you try

Find the mean of 3, 5, 7, 9.

Show full solution
Mean = 3 + 5 + 7 + 94
= 244
= 6

Median

Order the data first. For an odd number of values choose the middle; for an even number average the two middle values.

Odd (5 values)Even (4 values)
2, 4, 6, 8, 102, 4, 6, 8
Median = 6Median = 5

Worked example

Find the median of 9, 2, 6, 4.

Ordered: 2, 4, 6, 9
Median = 4 + 62
= 5

Now you try

Find the median of 8, 1, 5, 3, 9.

Show full solution
Ordered: 1, 3, 5, 8, 9
Middle value = 5

Mode and modality

The mode is the most frequent value. A dataset may have no mode (all values equally frequent), one mode (unimodal), two modes (bimodal) or multiple modes (multimodal).

Worked example

Find the modes of 1, 1, 2, 3, 3.

1 and 3 each occur twice.
2 occurs once.
Modes = 1 and 3 (bimodal).

Now you try

Find the mode of 4, 5, 5, 6.

Show full solution
5 occurs twice; other values occur once.
Mode = 5

Range

Range measures spread, not centre. Subtract the minimum from the maximum.

Worked example

Find the range of 3, 4, 5, 7, 9.

Range = maximum − minimum
= 9 − 3
= 6

Now you try

Find the range of 12, 8, 15, 10.

Show full solution
Range = 15 − 8
= 7

Comparing datasets

Compare typical values using centre and consistency using range. A smaller range means less spread between extremes.

Worked example

Compare the median and range.

Median A = 6; median B = 6
Range A = 8 − 4 = 4
Range B = 11 − 1 = 10
A has less spread.

Now you try

Which team has the higher mean?

Show full solution
A: 305 = 6
B: 305 = 6
Means are equal.

Clusters, gaps and outliers

Clusters are groups of nearby values; gaps are empty intervals; outliers are unusually distant observations. Consider the context before deciding whether a value is an error.

Worked example

Describe the distribution.

Most times cluster from 4 to 7 minutes.
There is a gap from 8 to 19.
20 is a possible outlier, perhaps due to traffic.

Now you try

What should you do before deleting 20?

Show full solution
Check whether it is a valid observation or recording error.

Adding and removing values

Adding values at the high end generally raises the mean; adding low values lowers it. Removing high values lowers the mean. Median and range may also change.

Worked example

Compare the means and medians.

Original mean = 204 = 5
New mean = 426 = 7
Original median = 5; new median = 7

Now you try

Remove 10 and 12. What happens to the range?

Show full solution
Before = 12 − 2 = 10
After = 8 − 2 = 6
Range decreases by 4.

Outliers and choosing a measure

Mean and range can be strongly affected by extreme values. Median is often more representative for skewed numerical data; mode is useful for categorical data.

Worked example

Compare the mean, median and range.

Original mean = 305 = 6
New mean = 525 = 10.4
Median stays 6.
Range changes from 4 to 26.

Now you try

Which centre best represents the new times?

Show full solution
Median = 6, because the extreme time pulls the mean upwards.

Shape of distributions

Symmetrical distributions have roughly balanced sides. Positive skew has a longer right tail; negative skew has a longer left tail.

Worked example

A distribution has most scores near 90 and a long tail towards low scores. Describe its shape.

Tail extends left towards lower values.
Negatively skewed.

Now you try

Most waiting times are short but a few are very long. Describe the shape.

Show full solution
Long tail towards higher values.
Positively skewed.

Census, samples and conclusions

A census studies every unit in a population. A sample studies a subset intended to represent the population. Use evidence and consider representativeness before drawing conclusions.

Worked example

Estimate the proportion who walk and explain a limitation.

Proportion = 2440 = 0.6 = 60%
This estimates the school proportion only if the sample is representative.

Now you try

Would surveying only students at the bike racks represent the whole school?

Show full solution
No. The location may overrepresent cyclists; sample students across the school.
