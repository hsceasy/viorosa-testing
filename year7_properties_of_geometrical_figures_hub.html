Naming and labelling figures

Name vertices in boundary order. AB denotes a side; ∠ABC has vertex B. Matching ticks indicate equal lengths, arrows indicate parallel sides.

Worked example

Name the side opposite AB in ABCD.

AB and CD share no vertex.
Opposite side = CD

Now you try

Name the angle at C in ABCD.

Show full solution
The arms are CB and CD.
∠BCD

Triangles by side length

Equilateral: three equal sides. Isosceles: at least two equal sides (the non-equilateral example has exactly two). Scalene: all sides different.

Worked example

An isosceles triangle has equal sides 9 cm and perimeter 25 cm. Find its base.

b + 9 + 9 = 25
b + 18 = 25
b = 25 − 18
b = 7 cm

Now you try

Equal sides are 8 cm and perimeter is 22 cm. Find the base.

Show full solution
b + 8 + 8 = 22
b + 16 = 22
b = 22 − 16
b = 6 cm

Triangles by angle

Acute: all angles below 90°. Right-angled: one angle is 90°. Obtuse: one angle exceeds 90°.

Worked example

Classify 35°, 55°, 90°.

90° is a right angle.
The triangle is right-angled.

Now you try

Classify 25°, 35°, 120°.

Show full solution
120° > 90°.
The triangle is obtuse-angled.

Triangle angle sum

Draw a line through A parallel to BC. The angles at B and C equal the alternate angles beside A. Together with angle A they form a straight angle, proving the sum is 180°.

Worked example

Find x if the other angles are 48° and 67°.

x + 48° + 67° = 180° (triangle angle sum)
x + 115° = 180°
x = 180° − 115°
x = 65°

Now you try

Find x if the other angles are 36° and 79°.

Show full solution
x + 36° + 79° = 180°
x + 115° = 180°
x = 65°

Exterior angles

An exterior angle and its adjacent interior angle sum to 180°. The three interior angles also sum to 180°. Subtract the adjacent interior angle from both equations to prove the exterior angle equals the two remote interior angles.

Worked example

Remote angles are 42° and 65°. Find exterior angle x.

x = 42° + 65° (exterior angle rule)
x = 107°

Now you try

Remote angles are 35° and 78°.

Show full solution
x = 35° + 78°
x = 113°

Isosceles and equilateral angles

Equal sides have equal opposite angles. An equilateral triangle has three equal angles.

Worked example

Vertex angle is 44°. Find each base angle x.

2x + 44° = 180°
2x = 136°
x = 136° ÷ 2
x = 68°

Now you try

Find each angle of an equilateral triangle.

Show full solution
3x = 180°
x = 180° ÷ 3
x = 60°

Convex and non-convex quadrilaterals

A convex quadrilateral has all interior angles less than 180°. A non-convex simple quadrilateral has a reflex interior angle.

Worked example

A quadrilateral has an interior angle of 210°. Classify it.

210° > 180°
It is non-convex.

Now you try

All four angles are 90°. Classify by convexity.

Show full solution
All angles are less than 180°.
It is convex.

Parallelograms, rectangles and squares

Parallelograms have opposite sides equal and parallel, opposite angles equal and diagonals that bisect each other. Rectangles also have four right angles and equal diagonals. Squares additionally have four equal sides and perpendicular diagonals.

Worked example

A parallelogram has an angle of 73°. Find an adjacent angle.

x + 73° = 180° (co-interior angles)
x = 180° − 73°
x = 107°

Now you try

An angle is 112°. Find its adjacent angle.

Show full solution
x + 112° = 180°
x = 68°

Rhombuses, kites and trapeziums

A rhombus has four equal sides, parallel opposite sides and perpendicular bisecting diagonals. A kite has two pairs of adjacent equal sides; its symmetry diagonal bisects the other at right angles. A trapezium has a pair of parallel sides (we use exactly one pair here).

Worked example

A rhombus has perimeter 36 cm. Find each side.

4s = 36
s = 36 ÷ 4
s = 9 cm

Now you try

A kite has adjacent sides 5 cm and 8 cm. Find perimeter.

Show full solution
P = 5 + 5 + 8 + 8
P = 26 cm

Quadrilateral classification

A square is both a rectangle and a rhombus, and both are parallelograms. A shape can belong to several groups. Verify properties by measuring sides, angles and diagonals; a sketch alone is not proof.

Worked example

Explain why every square is a rectangle.

A square has four right angles.
This satisfies the definition of a rectangle.

Now you try

Is every rectangle a square?

Show full solution
No. A rectangle may have unequal adjacent sides.
For example, 3 cm by 5 cm.

Quadrilateral angle sum

A diagonal divides a convex quadrilateral into two triangles. Each has angle sum 180°, so the quadrilateral sum is 360°.

Worked example

Find the fourth angle when the others are 80°, 95°, 105°.

x + 80° + 95° + 105° = 360°
x + 280° = 360°
x = 80°

Now you try

Other angles are 70°, 100°, 110°.

Show full solution
x + 70° + 100° + 110° = 360°
x + 280° = 360°
x = 80°

Multi-step geometrical reasoning

Use known properties first, write an equation, then solve and give a reason for every angle rule.

Worked example

Adjacent angles are x and 2x. Find both.

x + 2x = 180° (co-interior angles)
3x = 180°
x = 60°
2x = 120°

Now you try

Adjacent angles are x and 3x.

Show full solution
x + 3x = 180°
4x = 180°
x = 45°
3x = 135°
