Key terms
Point
An exact location.
Ray
Starts at a point and extends indefinitely in one direction.
Line
Extends indefinitely in both directions.
Interval
A segment with two endpoints.
Vertex
The common endpoint of two arms.
Angle
The turn between two rays.
Right angle
90°
Straight angle
180°
Reflex angle
Greater than 180° and less than 360°.
Revolution
360°
Adjacent angles
Share a vertex and arm without overlapping.
Complementary angles
Sum to 90°.
Supplementary angles
Sum to 180°.
Vertically opposite angles
Opposite angles at intersecting lines; equal.
Parallel
Lines in a plane that never meet (∥).
Perpendicular
Lines meeting at 90° (⊥).
Transversal
A line crossing two or more lines.
Corresponding angles
Same relative position at two intersections.
Alternate angle
On opposite sides of a transversal, inside the two lines in this lesson.
Co-interior angles
Inside the lines on the same side of the transversal.

Geometry notation

Name an angle with its vertex in the middle. A line extends both ways; a ray has one endpoint; an interval has two.

Worked example

Name the angle shown.

Vertex = B
Arms = BA and BC
Angle = ∠ABC (or ∠CBA)

Now you try

Name the angle with vertex Q and arms QP and QR.

Show full solution
Vertex = Q
Angle = ∠PQR

Classifying and measuring angles

Acute: greater than 0° and less than 90°. Right: 90°. Obtuse: between 90° and 180°. Straight: 180°. Reflex: between 180° and 360°. A full turn is 360°. Align a protractor centre with the vertex and its zero line with one arm.

Worked example

Classify 120°.

90° < 120° < 180°
Therefore obtuse.

Now you try

Classify 235°.

Show full solution
180° < 235° < 360°
Therefore reflex.

Complementary and adjacent angles

Adjacent angles share a vertex and arm. Complementary angles total 90°; they need not be adjacent.

Worked example

Find x in a right angle split into 35° and x.

x + 35° = 90° (complementary angles)
x = 90° − 35°
x = 55°

Now you try

Find the complement of 28°.

Show full solution
x + 28° = 90°
x = 90° − 28°
x = 62°

Supplementary angles

Angles forming a straight line sum to 180°. Supplementary angles need not be adjacent.

Worked example

Find x.

x + 65° = 180° (angles on a straight line)
x = 180° − 65°
x = 115°

Now you try

Find the supplement of 138°.

Show full solution
x + 138° = 180°
x = 180° − 138°
x = 42°

Angles around a point

A complete revolution is 360°.

Worked example

Find x.

x + 90° + 120° = 360° (angles around a point)
x + 210° = 360°
x = 360° − 210°
x = 150°

Now you try

Three angles around a point are 80°, 145° and x.

Show full solution
x + 80° + 145° = 360°
x + 225° = 360°
x = 360° − 225°
x = 135°

Vertically opposite angles

When two lines intersect, opposite angles are equal.

Worked example

Find x.

x = 60° (vertically opposite angles)

Now you try

An angle opposite 132° is x.

Show full solution
x = 132° (vertically opposite angles)

Parallel and perpendicular lines

Parallel lines are marked with matching arrows. Perpendicular lines meet at right angles. A transversal crosses two or more lines.

Worked example

Describe the relationships.

a ∥ b (matching arrows)
t ⊥ a and t ⊥ b (right-angle marks)

Now you try

If a line is perpendicular to a, what angle does it form with a?

Show full solution
90° (definition of perpendicular)

Corresponding angles

Corresponding angles occupy matching positions. They are equal when the lines are parallel.

Worked example

Find x.

x = 50° (corresponding angles, parallel lines)

Now you try

A corresponding angle to 116° on parallel lines is x.

Show full solution
x = 116° (corresponding angles)

Alternate angles

Alternate interior angles lie inside the lines on opposite sides of the transversal. They are equal for parallel lines.

Worked example

Find x.

x = 50° (alternate interior angles, parallel lines)

Now you try

Find the alternate interior angle to 72°.

Show full solution
x = 72° (alternate interior angles, parallel lines)

Co-interior angles

Co-interior angles lie inside the lines on the same side of the transversal. For parallel lines they sum to 180°.

Worked example

Find x.

x + 50° = 180° (co-interior angles, parallel lines)
x = 180° − 50°
x = 130°

Now you try

Find the co-interior angle to 108° on parallel lines.

Show full solution
x + 108° = 180°
x = 180° − 108°
x = 72°

Proving lines parallel

Equal corresponding or alternate angles, or supplementary co-interior angles, establish that two lines are parallel.

Worked example

Are the two lines parallel?

Corresponding angles = 50° and 50°
50° = 50°
Therefore the lines are parallel (converse of corresponding angle rule).

Now you try

Co-interior angles are 110° and 70°. Are the lines parallel?

Show full solution
110° + 70° = 180°
Therefore parallel (converse of co-interior angle rule).

Multi-step angle reasoning

Use one angle rule at a time and give a reason for every step.

Worked example

Find x and y.

x + 60° = 180° (straight line)
x = 180° − 60°
x = 120°
y = 60° (vertically opposite angles)

Now you try

Two intersecting lines form an angle of 74°. Find an adjacent and opposite angle.

Show full solution
Adjacent angle = 180° − 74°
Adjacent angle = 106° (straight line)
Opposite angle = 74° (vertically opposite angles)
