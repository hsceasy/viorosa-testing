Probability scale

Probability measures likelihood from 0 to 1 inclusive.

Worked example

Place 75% on the scale.

75% = 0.75
Likely

Now you try

Describe 0.25.

Show full solution
0.25 is between 0 and 0.5.
Unlikely.

Outcomes and sample spaces

An outcome is a possible result. A sample space lists all possible outcomes once.

Worked example

List the sample space for a standard die.

S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}

Now you try

List the sample space for a coin.

Show full solution
S = {heads, tails}

Events and equally likely outcomes

An event is a set of outcomes. Fair dice have six equally likely outcomes.

Worked example

List outcomes for rolling an even number.

E = {2, 4, 6}
There are 3 favourable outcomes.

Now you try

List outcomes greater than 4.

Show full solution
E = {5, 6}
There are 2 favourable outcomes.

Theoretical probability

For equally likely outcomes, divide favourable outcomes by total possible outcomes.

Worked example

Find P(A).

P(A) = 14
= 0.25

Now you try

Find P(A or B).

Show full solution
P(A or B) = 24
= 12

Fractions, decimals and percentages

Equivalent representations describe the same likelihood.

Worked example

Convert 3 out of 4 to a decimal and percentage.

P = 34
= 0.75
= 75%

Now you try

Convert 1 out of 5.

Show full solution
P = 15
= 0.2
= 20%

Total probability

Probabilities of mutually exclusive outcomes that cover the sample space sum to 1.

Worked example

Verify the total probability of the spinner.

Total = 14 + 14 + 14 + 14
= 44
= 1

Now you try

Three outcomes have probabilities 0.2, 0.3 and p. Find p.

Show full solution
p = 1 − 0.2 − 0.3
= 0.5

The complement contains every outcome not in the event.

Find P(not rolling even).

P(not even) = 36

Show full solution

Complement rule

An event and its complement have total probability 1.

Worked example

P(rain) = 0.3. Find P(no rain).

P(no rain) = 1 − 0.3
= 0.7

Now you try

P(win) = 0.65. Find P(not winning).

Show full solution
P(not winning) = 1 − 0.65
= 0.35

Expected frequency

Multiply theoretical probability by number of trials to predict the expected count, not a guaranteed result.

Worked example

Predict sixes in 60 fair die rolls.

Expected = 60 × 16
= 10

Now you try

Predict heads in 80 fair coin tosses.

Show full solution
Expected = 80 × 12
= 40

Must the next trial be a six if none occurred recently?
