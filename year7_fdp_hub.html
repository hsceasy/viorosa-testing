Key terms
Fraction
A number expressed as a quotient of two integers, with non-zero denominator.
Rational number
A number expressible as a ratio of integers with non-zero denominator.
Irrational number
A real number that is not rational.
Improper fraction
A fraction whose numerator is at least its denominator for positive fractions.
Decimal
A representation using base-ten place value.
Highest common factor (HCF)
Largest positive factor shared by two positive integers.
Percentage
A quantity expressed per hundred.
Numerator
Top number in a fraction.
Denominator
Bottom number in a fraction; never zero.
Recurring decimal
A decimal with a repeating block of digits.
Terminating decimal
A decimal with finitely many digits after the decimal point.
Number line
A line showing numbers in order.
Integer
A whole number, its negative, or zero.
Equivalent
Representing the same value.
Mixed number
A whole number and a proper fraction.
Lesson 01

Equivalent fractions and HCF

List factors to find the highest common factor. Divide both numerator and denominator by the HCF to simplify. Multiply both by the same non-zero number to create an equivalent fraction.

Worked example

Simplify 1824.

HCF(18,24) = 6, so 1824 = 34.

Now you try

Simplify 2135.

Show worked solution

HCF = 7; 2135 = 35.

Lesson 02

Comparing fractions and mixed numbers

Convert to a common denominator or decimal. A mixed number combines a whole number and a fraction; multiply the whole part by the denominator and add the numerator to convert to an improper fraction.

Worked example

Compare 56 and 79.

1518 > 1418, so 56 > 79.

Now you try

Convert 2 35 to an improper fraction.

Show worked solution

(2 × 5 + 3) = 13, so 135.

Lesson 03

Decimal place value and rounding

Locate the required decimal place. If the next digit is 5 or greater, round up; otherwise keep the digit. Use ≈ for an approximation. Keep exact values during calculations where possible.

Worked example

Round 8.376 to two decimal places.

8.376 ≈ 8.38.

Now you try

Round 0.0964 to two decimal places.

Show worked solution

0.0964 ≈ 0.10.

Lesson 04

Terminating, recurring and irrational numbers

A terminating decimal ends; a recurring decimal repeats a fixed block forever. A bar over the repeating digits indicates recurrence. Rational numbers are ratios of integers with non-zero denominator; terminating and recurring decimals are rational. Irrational numbers cannot be expressed this way.

Worked example

Classify 0.125, 0.333… and √2.

The first two are rational; √2 is irrational and lies between 1.4 and 1.5.

Now you try

Use a calculator to locate √5 between consecutive tenths.

Show worked solution

√5 ≈ 2.236, between 2.2 and 2.3.

Lesson 05

Converting fractions, decimals and percentages

Divide numerator by denominator to get a decimal. Multiply a decimal by 100 to express it as a percentage. Divide a percentage by 100 to obtain a decimal. Use place value to turn a terminating decimal into a fraction.

Worked example

Convert 78 to a decimal and percentage.

7 ÷ 8 = 0.875 = 87.5%.

Now you try

Convert 0.36 to a fraction and percentage.

Show worked solution

36100 = 925; 36%.

Lesson 06

Ordering signed numbers and FDP

Convert to a common representation, then place numbers on a number line. Values increase to the right; negative numbers farther from zero are smaller.

Worked example

Order −0.4, −12, 25% and 0.3.

−12 < −0.4 < 25% < 0.3.

Now you try

Order 0.7, 34 and 68%.

Show worked solution

68% < 0.7 < 34.

Lesson 07

Adding and subtracting fractions

Use a common denominator, then add or subtract numerators. Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions when helpful. A whole number can be written as a fraction with the required denominator.

Worked example

Calculate 2 − 35.

105 − 35 = 75 = 1 25.

Now you try

Calculate 1 14 + 23.

Show worked solution

54 + 23 = 1512 + 812 = 2312.

Lesson 08

Multiplying and dividing fractions

Multiply numerators and denominators. To divide, multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor. Convert mixed numbers first. Multiplying a positive quantity by a number between zero and one makes it smaller; dividing makes it larger.

Worked example

Calculate 34 ÷ 25.

34 × 52 = 158.

Now you try

Calculate 1 12 × 23.

Show worked solution

32 × 23 = 1.

Lesson 09

Decimal operations

Align decimal points for addition and subtraction. For multiplication, count decimal places in both factors. For division, scale both numbers by the same power of ten to make the divisor a whole number. Estimate to check.

Worked example

Calculate 4.2 ÷ 0.6.

42 ÷ 6 = 7.

Now you try

Calculate 0.24 × 0.3.

Show worked solution

24 × 3 = 72; three decimal places gives 0.072.

Lesson 10

Fractions and percentages of quantities

Multiply the quantity by the fraction or decimal equivalent of the percentage. To express one quantity as a fraction of another, use matching units and divide part by whole.

Worked example

Express 30 cm as a percentage of 2 m.

2 m = 200 cm; 30200 = 0.15 = 15%.

Now you try

Find 35% of 80.

Show worked solution

0.35 × 80 = 28.

Lesson 11

Percentage change and financial maths

Percentage change = change ÷ original × 100%. Increase by multiplying by 1 plus the decimal rate; decrease by 1 minus the rate. For profit or loss, specify the base price. Australian GST is 10% on taxable sales.

Worked example

A $80 item is reduced by 15%. Find the sale price.

$80 × 0.85 = $68.

Now you try

A shop buys an item for $50 and sells it for $65. Find profit as a percentage of cost.

Show worked solution

$15 ÷ $50 × 100% = 30%.

Lesson 12

Unitary method and problem solving

Find 1% by dividing a known percentage amount by its percentage number, then multiply to find the required percentage. For GST-inclusive prices, divide by 1.10 to recover the pre-GST price.

Worked example

If 30% of a number is 18, find the number.

1% = 18 ÷ 30 = 0.6; 100% = 60.

Now you try

A GST-inclusive price is $132. Find the pre-GST price.

Show worked solution

$132 ÷ 1.10 = $120.
