Lesson 01

Sample spaces

List all possible outcomes without repetition.

Worked example

List outcomes of a fair six-sided die.

S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}
There are 6 outcomes.

Now you try

How many outcomes for a fair coin?

Show worked solution

S = {H, T}
Count = 2

Lesson 02

Theoretical probability

For equally likely outcomes, divide favourable outcomes by total outcomes.

Worked example

Find probability of rolling an even number.

Even outcomes: 2, 4, 6
P = 36
= 12

Now you try

Find probability of rolling greater than 4.

Show worked solution

Outcomes: 5, 6
P = 26
= 13

Lesson 03

Complementary events

An event and its complement cover all outcomes and cannot occur together.

Worked example

P(rain) = 0.3. Find P(no rain).

P(no rain) = 1 − 0.3
= 0.7

Now you try

P(win) = 0.25. Find P(not win).

Show worked solution

1 − 0.25
= 0.75

Lesson 04

Unequal spinner sectors

For a fair spinner, probability is proportional to central angle.

Worked example

Find probability of a 90° sector.

P = 90360
= 14

Now you try

Find probability of a 120° sector.

Show worked solution

P = 120360
= 13

Lesson 05

Two-stage experiments

Use a table or organised list to record all ordered outcomes.

Worked example

Toss two fair coins. Find probability of exactly one head.

S = {HH, HT, TH, TT}
Favourable: HT, TH
P = 24
= 12

Two coin tosses
HT
HHHHT
TTHTT

Now you try

Find probability of two heads.

Show worked solution

Favourable: HH
P = 14

Lesson 06

Tables of outcomes

Use rows and columns to list outcomes of two independent trials.

Worked example

Roll two fair dice. Find probability of sum 2.

Total = 6 × 6 = 36
Only (1, 1) gives sum 2
P = 136

Now you try

Find probability of sum 12.

Show worked solution

Only (6, 6) gives sum 12
P = 136

Lesson 07

Mutually exclusive events

Events that cannot occur together have probabilities that add for either event.

Worked example

Find P(1 or 6) on a fair die.

P = 16 + 16
= 26
= 13

Now you try

Find P(2 or 3) on a fair die.

Show worked solution

P = 16 + 16
= 26
= 13

Lesson 08

Experimental probability

Relative frequency is observed successes divided by trials.

Worked example

A coin lands heads 18 times in 40 tosses.

P ≈ 1840
= 920
= 0.45

Now you try

A spinner lands red 12 times in 30 spins.

Show worked solution

P ≈ 1230
= 25
= 0.4

Lesson 09

Expected frequency

Multiply probability by number of trials to estimate frequency.

Worked example

Expect sixes in 120 fair die rolls.

Expected = 120 × 16
= 1206
= 20

Now you try

Expect heads in 80 fair coin tosses.

Show worked solution

Expected = 80 × 12
= 40

Lesson 10

Simulations

Use repeated random trials to model chance. Results vary.

Worked example

A simulation gives 48 successes in 100 trials. Compare with probability 0.5.

Observed = 48100 = 0.48
Difference = 0.5 − 0.48 = 0.02

Now you try

Find observed probability for 55 successes in 100 trials.

Show worked solution

P ≈ 55100
= 0.55

Lesson 11

Long-run relative frequency

With many independent trials, relative frequency tends towards theoretical probability, but is not guaranteed to equal it.

Worked example

A fair coin gives 6 heads in 10 tosses. Is it necessarily biased?

Observed = 610 = 0.6
Small samples vary.
No.

Now you try

Does 51 heads in 100 fair tosses prove bias?

Show worked solution

Observed = 0.51
Variation is expected.
No.

Lesson 12

Comparing chance models

Check assumptions about fairness and equally likely outcomes.

Worked example

Bag contains 3 red and 7 blue counters. Find P(red).

Total = 3 + 7 = 10
P(red) = 310
= 0.3

Now you try

Bag has 4 red and 6 blue. Find P(blue).

Show worked solution

Total = 4 + 6 = 10
P(blue) = 610
= 0.6
