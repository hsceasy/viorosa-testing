Direct and inverse variation

Direct and inverse variation

Direct variation means y = kx: doubling x doubles y. The graph passes through the origin.

Inverse variation means y = k/x: doubling a nonzero x halves y. The product xy stays constant.

The constant k is fixed for the situation. Inverse variation excludes x = 0.

Steps for direct variation

  1. Write y = kx.
  2. Substitute the known x and y values.
  3. Divide by x to find k.
  4. Rewrite the equation with k, then substitute the new value and solve.

Worked example — direct variation

y varies directly with x. When x = 3, y = 18. Find y when x = 9.

Solution

\[y=kx\]

\[18=3k\]

\[k=\frac{18}{3}=6\]

\[y=6x\]

\[y=6(9)=54\]

Steps for inverse variation

  1. Write y = k/x.
  2. Substitute the known x and y values.
  3. Multiply by x to find k.
  4. Rewrite the equation with k, then substitute the new value and solve.

Worked example — inverse variation

y varies inversely with x. When x = 3, y = 18. Find y when x = 9.

Solution

\[y=\frac{k}{x}\]

\[18=\frac{k}{3}\]

\[k=18(3)=54\]

\[y=\frac{54}{x}\]

\[y=\frac{54}{9}=6\]

Graph transformations

Changing k stretches or compresses the graph vertically; negative k reflects it in the x-axis. Adding a vertical or horizontal shift generally changes the original direct or inverse variation relationship, so do not introduce shifts when fitting a pure variation model.
