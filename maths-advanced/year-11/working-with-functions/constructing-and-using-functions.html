Constructing and using functions

Key ideas

A model is an equation that describes a real situation. Choose variables and realistic restrictions before writing it.

Linear models describe constant rates. Simultaneous equations find where two quantities are equal. Inequalities describe limits or constraints.

Break-even occurs when revenue equals cost. Profit is revenue minus cost.

Quadratic models can describe a maximum or minimum. Always interpret the result in context.

Building and solving a model

  1. Define variables and units.
  2. Write the rule or rules from the information given.
  3. Solve the equation or inequality.
  4. Check restrictions and explain the answer in context.

Worked example — break-even

A business pays $120 plus $4 per item and sells each item for $10. Find break-even and when profit is positive.

Solution

\[C=120+4n,\quad R=10n\]

\[120+4n=10n\]

\[n=20\]

\[P=6n-120>0\]

\[n>20\]

At least 21 items must be sold for a profit.

Worked example — maximum area

A rectangle has perimeter 20 m. Find its maximum area.

Solution

\[A=x(10-x)=-(x-5)^2+25\]

The vertex is (5, 25). The maximum area is 25 m² for a 5 m by 5 m square.

Graph transformations

Increasing a fixed cost shifts the cost line up. Increasing cost per item makes the line steeper. In a quadratic model the vertex moves when the horizontal or vertical shift changes. Only use the portion of the graph meaningful in context.

Adding a number outside the function moves the graph up; subtracting moves it down.

Replacing x with x − h moves the graph right h units; replacing x with x + h moves it left h units (h positive).

Multiplying the output by 2 doubles its height from the x-axis; multiplying by ½ halves it. A negative multiplier also reflects it in the x-axis.

Replacing x by 2x compresses horizontally by factor ½; replacing x by x/2 stretches horizontally by factor 2. Replacing x by −x reflects in the y-axis.
