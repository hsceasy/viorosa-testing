Circles and semicircles

Key ideas

Every point on a circle is the same distance from its centre. For a circle centred at the origin, Pythagoras gives x² + y² = r².

A full circle is a relation, not a function of x, because most vertical lines through it meet two points.

Taking the positive square root gives the upper semicircle; the negative square root gives the lower semicircle. Solving for x gives right and left semicircles.

Sketching a circle

  1. Identify the centre and radius.
  2. Plot the four extreme points one radius away from the centre.
  3. Draw a smooth circle, or only the requested half.

Worked example

Find the semicircle equations for a circle of radius 5 centred at the origin.

Solution

\[x^2+y^2=25\]

\[y=\sqrt{25-x^2}\quad\text{(upper)}\]

\[y=-\sqrt{25-x^2}\quad\text{(lower)}\]

\[x=\sqrt{25-y^2}\quad\text{(right)}\]

\[x=-\sqrt{25-y^2}\quad\text{(left)}\]

The upper semicircle has domain [−5, 5] and range [0, 5].

Graph transformations

A circle (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r² has centre (h, k). Increasing r enlarges it equally in both directions. Stretching only one axis makes an ellipse, not a circle. Reflecting an upper semicircle in the x-axis gives the lower semicircle.
