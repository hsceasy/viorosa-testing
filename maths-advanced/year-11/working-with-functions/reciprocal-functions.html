Reciprocal functions

Key ideas

A reciprocal function y = k/x has two branches and is undefined at x = 0.

The lines x = 0 and y = 0 are asymptotes: the branches approach these lines.

If k is positive the branches lie in quadrants I and III; if negative, quadrants II and IV.

As |x| increases, y approaches zero. As x approaches zero, |y| grows without bound.

Sketching a reciprocal graph

  1. Identify k and its sign.
  2. Draw the asymptotes as dashed lines.
  3. Calculate points on both sides of the vertical asymptote.
  4. Sketch two separate branches approaching the asymptotes.

Worked example

Describe y = 6/x.

Solution

\[x\to0^+\implies y\to+\infty\]

\[x\to-\infty\implies y\to0^-\]

Domain and range both exclude zero.

Graph transformations

For y = a/(x − h) + k, the asymptotes move to x = h and y = k. For example y = 2/(x − 1) + 2 shifts the reciprocal right 1 and up 2 after a vertical stretch by 2.

Adding a number outside the function moves the graph up; subtracting moves it down.

Replacing x with x − h moves the graph right h units; replacing x with x + h moves it left h units (h positive).

Multiplying the output by 2 doubles its height from the x-axis; multiplying by ½ halves it. A negative multiplier also reflects it in the x-axis.

Replacing x by 2x compresses horizontally by factor ½; replacing x by x/2 stretches horizontally by factor 2. Replacing x by −x reflects in the y-axis.
