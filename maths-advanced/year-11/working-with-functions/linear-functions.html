Linear functions

Key ideas

A linear function has a constant gradient and a straight-line graph. In y = mx + c, m is the gradient and c is the y-intercept.

General form is Ax + By + C = 0. Point–gradient form is y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).

Parallel nonvertical lines have equal gradients. Perpendicular nonvertical lines have gradients with product −1. Horizontal and vertical lines are perpendicular.

Find the x-intercept by setting y = 0; find the y-intercept by setting x = 0.

When multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative number, reverse the inequality sign.

Finding a line through two points

  1. Find the gradient using change in y divided by change in x.
  2. Substitute the gradient and either point into point–gradient form.
  3. Expand and rearrange to the requested form.

Worked example

Find the line through (2, 1) and (4, 7).

Solution

\[m=\frac{7-1}{4-2}=3\]

\[y-1=3(x-2)\]

\[y=3x-5\]

\[3x-y-5=0\]

Worked example — inequality

Solve −2x + 4 > 10.

Solution

\[-2x>6\]

\[x<-3\]

On a number line use an open circle at −3 and shade left.

Graph transformations

For y = x, y = 2x is steeper; y = ½x is flatter. y = −x reflects the original in the x-axis. y = x + 2 moves it up 2. A horizontal shift of a straight line can also be described as a vertical shift.

Adding a number outside the function moves the graph up; subtracting moves it down.

Replacing x with x − h moves the graph right h units; replacing x with x + h moves it left h units (h positive).

Multiplying the output by 2 doubles its height from the x-axis; multiplying by ½ halves it. A negative multiplier also reflects it in the x-axis.

Replacing x by 2x compresses horizontally by factor ½; replacing x by x/2 stretches horizontally by factor 2. Replacing x by −x reflects in the y-axis.
