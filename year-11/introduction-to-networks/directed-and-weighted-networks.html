Directed and weighted networks

Arrows show permitted travel direction; weights represent quantities such as kilometres. In the directed example A → B → C has weight 2 + 3 = 5, less than the direct A → C edge of weight 8.

Reading a more realistic road network

A vertex represents a junction. An arrow permits travel only in its direction. A weight gives a quantity, here minutes. Two opposite arrows can have different weights.

From A to F, A–B–D–F takes 4 + 5 + 6 = 15 minutes. A–C–E–F takes 2 + 7 + 3 = 12 minutes. A–C–B–D–E–F takes 2 + 1 + 5 + 2 + 3 = 13 minutes. The reverse of any of these routes is not automatically permitted. D to E takes 2 minutes but E to D takes 4.
