VIOROSA MATHS · YEAR 11 · MATHEMATICS STANDARD

Connected and Eulerian networks

Key idea

A connected undirected network has an Euler circuit when every vertex has even degree, and an open Euler trail when exactly two vertices have odd degree.

Worked example 1

Triangle degrees 2,2,2: Euler circuit exists.

Worked example 2

Chain A–B–C degrees 1,2,1: open Euler trail from A to C.

Common mistake: Ignoring connectivity.

Connectivity and Euler trails

A network is connected when every pair of vertices can be joined by a path. A disconnected network has separate components.

Euler circuit: all degrees even

Trace A–B–C–D–A. Every edge is used exactly once and the start and finish coincide.

Open Euler trail: exactly two odd vertices

Trace A–B–C–D–A–C. Start and finish at the two odd vertices. A street sweeper can cover every road once but cannot return to the start without repeating a road.

Neither: four odd vertices

No Euler trail exists. More than two odd vertices require repeated edges if every road is to be covered. Connectivity is required for all vertices incident to edges.
