VIOROSA MATHS · YEAR 11 · MATHEMATICS STANDARD

Blood alcohol content (BAC)

Key idea

BAC is blood alcohol concentration in grams per 100 mL. These simplified classroom models cannot determine actual BAC or when it is safe or legal to drive.

BAC model (female coefficient)

B = 10N − 7.5H5.5M

N is standard drinks, H is elapsed hours, M is mass in kilograms.

BAC model (male coefficient)

B = 10N − 7.5H6.8M

Use the coefficient specified in the question; real individual variation is not captured.

Modelled elimination time

t = B0.015

t is the estimated time in hours under the simplified model, not a driving clearance.

Worked example 1 — interpreting a scenario

Question: A fictional dataset records four standard drinks over three hours for a person of mass 70 kg. Using the female-coefficient model, calculate the estimated BAC to four decimal places.

Solution

B = 10N − 7.5H5.5M

B = 10(4) − 7.5(3)5.5(70)

B = 40 − 22.5385

B = 17.5385

B ≈ 0.0455

Worked example 2 — rearranging the model

Question: A fictional data record lists five standard drinks, mass 75 kg and modelled BAC 0.05 using the male coefficient. The elapsed time field is missing. Find its value to one decimal place.

Solution

0.05 = 10(5) − 7.5H6.8(75)

0.05 = 50 − 7.5H510

0.05 × 510 = 50 − 7.5H

25.5 = 50 − 7.5H

25.5 − 50 = −7.5H

−24.5 = −7.5H

H = −24.5−7.5

H ≈ 3.3 hours

Worked example 3 — interpreting the time formula

Question: A classroom simulation starts with a modelled BAC of 0.060. Under the assumed constant elimination rate, calculate the modelled time to zero.

Solution

t = B0.015

t = 0.0600.015

t = 4 hours

This is a mathematical result only, not evidence of actual BAC or fitness to drive.

Check units, brackets and each inverse operation before finalising your answer.
