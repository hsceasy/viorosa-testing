Probability scale and equally likely outcomes

Probability ranges from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). Count outcomes only when equally likely. Convert fractions to decimals by division and decimals to percentages by multiplying by 100.

Read lesson →

Sample spaces and arrays

A sample space lists every possible outcome. An array lists ordered pairs systematically. For two stages with m and n choices, there are m × n possible pairs.

Read lesson →

Complementary events

An event and its complement are mutually exclusive and together cover the sample space. At least one is the complement of none.

Read lesson →

Relative frequency and long-run behaviour

Relative frequency is observed occurrences divided by trials. With repeated independent trials under stable conditions it tends to approach the underlying probability, but need not get closer at every step.

Read lesson →

Expected frequency

Expected frequency is the long-run average count, not a guaranteed result. Use a theoretical probability or a justified estimate.

Read lesson →

Probability trees

Label branches with outcomes and conditional probabilities. Branches from each node sum to 1. Multiply along a path and add distinct successful paths.

Read lesson →

Independent and dependent events

Independent events do not change each other’s probability. Without replacement, probabilities usually change.

Read lesson →

Multistage probability

Multiply along a path, add mutually exclusive paths, and use complements for at least one success.

Read lesson →

Venn diagrams

Start with the intersection, subtract it from each group total, then find neither. The union includes both groups and their overlap.

Read lesson →

Two-way tables

Organise two attributes using rows and columns. Marginal totals describe one attribute; interior cells describe both. Use the relevant group total for a probability restricted to that group.

Read lesson →

Probability in decision-making

Media, governments and companies use probability estimates to communicate risk and allocate resources. Check sample size, representativeness, absolute versus relative risk and uncertainty.
