Lesson 01

Index notation

The base is the repeated factor; the index tells how many factors.

Worked example

Evaluate 3⁴.

3⁴ = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3
= 9 × 9
= 81

Now you try

Evaluate 2⁵.

Show worked solution

2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2
= 4 × 4 × 2
= 32

Lesson 02

Powers of ten

A positive integer power of ten has that many zeros after 1.

Worked example

Evaluate 10⁴.

10⁴ = 10 × 10 × 10 × 10
= 100 × 100
= 10 000

Now you try

Evaluate 10³.

Show worked solution

10³ = 10 × 10 × 10
= 100 × 10
= 1000

Lesson 03

Divisibility and prime factors

Tests: 2 even; 3 digit sum divisible by 3; 4 last two digits divisible by 4; 5 ends in 0 or 5; 6 divisible by 2 and 3; 10 ends in 0. Break composite numbers into primes.

Worked example

Write 72 as prime powers.

72 = 8 × 9
= 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3
= 2³ × 3²

Now you try

Write 60 as prime powers.

Show worked solution

60 = 6 × 10
= 2 × 3 × 2 × 5
= 2² × 3 × 5

Lesson 04

Squares and square roots

A square root reverses squaring; √ denotes the nonnegative root.

Worked example

Evaluate √81.

9 × 9 = 81
√81 = 9

Now you try

Evaluate √144.

Show worked solution

12 × 12 = 144
√144 = 12

Lesson 05

Cubes and cube roots

A cube root reverses cubing.

Worked example

Evaluate ∛125.

5 × 5 × 5 = 125
∛125 = 5

Now you try

Evaluate ∛64.

Show worked solution

4 × 4 × 4 = 64
∛64 = 4

Lesson 06

Estimating roots

Bracket a non-perfect root between consecutive whole numbers, then check with a calculator.

Worked example

Estimate √30 and ∛30.

5² = 25, 6² = 36
√30 is between 5 and 6
√30 ≈ 5.48
3³ = 27, 4³ = 64
∛30 is between 3 and 4
∛30 ≈ 3.11

Now you try

Between which integers is √50?

Show worked solution

7² = 49
8² = 64
49 < 50 < 64
7 < √50 < 8

Lesson 07

Multiplication law

For the same base, add indices when multiplying.

Worked example

Simplify 2³ × 2⁴.

= (2 × 2 × 2) × (2 × 2 × 2 × 2)
= 2⁽³⁺⁴⁾
= 2⁷

Now you try

Simplify 5² × 5³.

Show worked solution

= (5 × 5) × (5 × 5 × 5)
= 5⁽²⁺³⁾
= 5⁵

Lesson 08

Division law

For the same nonzero base, subtract indices when dividing.

Worked example

Simplify 3⁵ ÷ 3².

= 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 33 × 3
= 3 × 3 × 3
= 3⁽⁵⁻²⁾
= 3³

Now you try

Simplify 7⁴ ÷ 7².

Show worked solution

= 7 × 7 × 7 × 77 × 7
= 7 × 7
= 7²

Lesson 09

Power of a power

Multiply indices when raising a power to another power.

Worked example

Simplify (2³)².

= 2³ × 2³
= (2 × 2 × 2) × (2 × 2 × 2)
= 2⁶

Now you try

Simplify (3²)³.

Show worked solution

= 3² × 3² × 3²
= 3⁽²⁺²⁺²⁾
= 3⁶

Lesson 10

Zero index

Any nonzero base raised to zero equals 1.

Worked example

Explain 4⁰.

4³4³ = 1
4³4³ = 4⁽³⁻³⁾
= 4⁰
Therefore 4⁰ = 1.

Now you try

Evaluate 9⁰.

Show worked solution

9⁰ = 1

Lesson 11

Negative bases and order of operations

Brackets determine whether a negative sign is part of the base. Evaluate powers before multiplication and addition.

Worked example

Compare (−3)² and −3².

(−3)² = (−3) × (−3)
= 9
−3² = −(3 × 3)
= −9

Now you try

Evaluate 2 + (−2)³ × 3.

Show worked solution

= 2 + (−8) × 3
= 2 − 24
= −22

Lesson 12

Mixed powers and roots

Apply index laws and order of operations. Negative indices are extension only: for nonzero a, a⁻ⁿ is the reciprocal of aⁿ.

Worked example

Evaluate √49 + 2³ × 2².

= 7 + 2⁽³⁺²⁾
= 7 + 2⁵
= 7 + 32
= 39

Now you try

Evaluate ∛27 + 3² × 3.

Show worked solution

= 3 + 3³
= 3 + 27
= 30
