Key terms
Operation
A calculation such as addition or division.
Commutative property
Changing order does not change a sum or product.
Factor
A quantity multiplied by another.
Factorise
Write an expression as a product of factors.
Pronumeral
A letter representing a number.
Pattern
A sequence following a rule.
Algebraic expression
A combination of numbers, letters and operations.
Associative law
Regrouping addition or multiplication preserves the result.
Distributive law
Multiply each term in a sum by the outside factor.
Coefficient
The numerical multiplier of a term.
Constant
A term without a variable.
Like terms
Terms with identical variable parts.
Lesson 01

Algebraic notation

A pronumeral is a letter representing a number. Omit the multiplication sign between a number and a letter.

Worked example

Write 4 × n + 3.

4n + 3

Now you try

Write 7 × p − 2.

Show worked solution

7p − 2

Lesson 02

Terms, coefficients and constants

Terms are separated by addition or subtraction. A coefficient multiplies a pronumeral; a constant has no pronumeral.

Worked example

Identify the terms in 5x − 8.

5x and −8; coefficient 5, constant −8.

Now you try

Identify the coefficient of −3y.

Show worked solution

−3

Lesson 03

Number properties

Commutative means swapping order; associative means regrouping. Both apply to addition and multiplication, not subtraction or division.

Worked example

Rewrite 2 + (x + 5).

(2 + 5) + x = 7 + x.

Now you try

Rewrite 3 × (2 × x).

Show worked solution

6x

Lesson 04

Writing expressions

Translate words into operations. Use brackets when an operation applies to a whole sum.

Worked example

Write twice the sum of n and 4.

2(n + 4)

Now you try

Write 5 less than three times p.

Show worked solution

3p − 5

Lesson 05

Substitution

Replace each letter with its value. Use brackets around negative values and follow order of operations.

Worked example

Evaluate 3x + 2 when x = −4.

3(−4) + 2 = −10.

Now you try

Evaluate 2a − 5 when a = 6.

Show worked solution

7

Lesson 06

Number patterns

Substitute consecutive position numbers into a rule to generate terms.

Worked example

Find the first three terms of 4n + 1 starting at n = 1.

5, 9, 13

Now you try

Find the first three terms of 3n − 2.

Show worked solution

1, 4, 7

Lesson 07

Collecting like terms

Like terms have identical variable parts, including powers. Add or subtract their coefficients.

Worked example

Simplify 7x − 2x + 3y.

5x + 3y

Now you try

Simplify 8a + 2b − 3a.

Show worked solution

5a + 2b

Lesson 08

Multiplying terms

Multiply coefficients and then variable factors. Repeated factors can be written as powers.

Worked example

Simplify 3x × 4x.

12x²

Now you try

Simplify −2a × 5b.

Show worked solution

−10ab

Lesson 09

Dividing terms

Divide coefficients and cancel common nonzero factors.

Worked example

Simplify 18x²6x, x ≠ 0.

3x

Now you try

Simplify 20ab5a, a ≠ 0.

Show worked solution

4b

Lesson 10

Expanding brackets

Distribute the outside factor to every term inside the brackets.

Worked example

Expand −3(x − 4).

−3x + 12

Now you try

Expand 5(2y − 3).

Show worked solution

10y − 15

Lesson 11

Factorising

Find the highest common factor and write it outside brackets. Check by expanding.

Worked example

Factorise 12x + 18.

6(2x + 3)

Now you try

Factorise 15a − 10.

Show worked solution

5(3a − 2)

Lesson 12

Applications and reasoning

Write an expression for a situation, then simplify or substitute as needed.

Worked example

Three tickets cost $x each plus a $4 booking fee.

Total = 3x + 4 dollars.

Now you try

A rectangle has sides x + 2 and 5. Write its perimeter.

Show worked solution

2(x + 2) + 10 = 2x + 14.

Explore substitution

Evaluate 3x + 2.
