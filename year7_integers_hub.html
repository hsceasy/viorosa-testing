Lesson 01

Direction and magnitude

Integers are whole numbers, their negatives and zero. Positive and negative signs describe direction relative to zero. Magnitude is distance from zero, always non-negative: |−6| = 6. Zero is neither positive nor negative.

Worked example

A diver is 6 m below sea level and a bird is 4 m above it. Give their signed positions and magnitudes.

Diver: −6 m, magnitude 6 m. Bird: +4 m, magnitude 4 m.

Now you try

A lift is three floors below ground. State its position and magnitude.

Show worked solution

Position −3; magnitude 3 floors.

Lesson 02

Number lines and ordering

On a number line, values increase to the right. Negative numbers farther from zero are smaller. The symbols < and > mean less than and greater than; the open end faces the larger value.

Worked example

Temperatures are −4°C, 2°C, −7°C and 0°C. Order from coldest to warmest.

−7 < −4 < 0 < 2. The order is −7°C, −4°C, 0°C, 2°C.

Now you try

Order −2°C, −9°C and 3°C; compare the two negative temperatures.

Show worked solution

−9°C, −2°C, 3°C; −9 < −2.

Lesson 03

Adding integers

Addition combines signed changes. Adding a positive moves right; adding a negative moves left. Same signs: add magnitudes and keep the sign. Different signs: subtract the smaller magnitude from the larger and keep the sign of the larger magnitude.

Worked example

A freezer starts at −8°C and warms by 11°C. Find its temperature.

−8 + 11 = 3. Final temperature 3°C.

Now you try

A diver at −4 m descends another 7 m. Find the new position.

Show worked solution

−4 + (−7) = −11 m.

Lesson 04

Subtracting integers

Subtraction means adding the opposite: a − b = a + (−b). The sign attached to a number describes position or direction; a sign between numbers describes an operation. Subtracting a negative moves right.

Worked example

The morning temperature is −5°C and the afternoon temperature is 4°C. Find the increase.

Final minus initial: 4 − (−5) = 4 + 5 = 9°C.

Now you try

A team has −3 points after a penalty. A penalty of 6 points is cancelled. Find the new score.

Show worked solution

−3 − (−6) = −3 + 6 = 3 points.

Lesson 05

Directed number sentences

Choose a positive direction and a zero reference. Translate each change into a signed integer, then write a number sentence. A negative balance is a debt; a positive change may reduce that debt.

Worked example

An account starts $12 overdrawn, receives $20 and pays $5. Find the balance.

−12 + 20 − 5 = 8 − 5 = 3. Balance $3.

Now you try

A lift starts on floor −2, rises 6 floors and descends 3. Where does it stop?

Show worked solution

−2 + 6 − 3 = 4 − 3 = 1. Floor 1.

Lesson 06

Multiplying integers

Multiplication of a negative integer by a positive whole number can be represented by repeated addition: 3 × (−4) = (−4) + (−4) + (−4) = −12. Same signs give a positive product; different signs give a negative product. Multiplying by zero gives zero. The pattern 2 × (−3) = −6, 1 × (−3) = −3, 0 × (−3) = 0, (−1) × (−3) = 3 explains the negative-times-negative rule.

Worked example

A submarine descends 5 m each minute for 4 minutes. Find its signed change.

4 × (−5) = −20 m, a descent of 20 m.

Now you try

Three penalties of 8 points are applied. Find the total change.

Show worked solution

3 × (−8) = −24 points.

Lesson 07

Dividing integers

Division reverses multiplication. Same signs give a positive quotient; different signs give a negative quotient. Zero divided by a nonzero number is zero; division by zero is undefined. Not every division of integers produces an integer.

Worked example

A total change of −36 m occurs evenly over 6 minutes. Find the change per minute.

−36 ÷ 6 = −6 m per minute. Check: 6 × (−6) = −36.

Now you try

A loss of 45 points is shared equally across 5 rounds. Find the signed change per round.

Show worked solution

−45 ÷ 5 = −9 points per round.

Lesson 08

Grouping symbols and powers

Evaluate grouping symbols first, then powers, then multiplication and division from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right. A negative base needs parentheses: (−3)² = 9, but −3² = −9.

Worked example

A game awards the square of a signed score change of −4. How many bonus points are awarded?

(−4)² = (−4) × (−4) = 16 points.

Now you try

A game subtracts the square of 5 from a score of zero. Find the score.

Show worked solution

0 − 5² = 0 − 25 = −25 points.

Lesson 09

Mixed operations

Use the order of operations even when integers are negative. Brackets can change the answer: 12 − 3 × 2 = 6, whereas (12 − 3) × 2 = 18. Estimate the sign and magnitude before calculating.

Worked example

A quiz starts with 10 points. Three incorrect answers cost 4 points each, then a bonus adds 5 points. Find the score.

10 + 3 × (−4) + 5 = 10 − 12 + 5 = −2 + 5 = 3 points.

Now you try

A player begins with −6 points and earns 4 points in each of 5 rounds, then loses 3 points. Find the score.

Show worked solution

−6 + 5 × 4 − 3 = −6 + 20 − 3 = 14 − 3 = 11 points.

Lesson 10

Problem solving and digital checks

Identify the reference point, choose signed values, write a directed number sentence and interpret the answer in context. Calculate by hand first, then use a calculator to check. Use parentheses around negative numbers and distinguish the negative key from subtraction. For example, check (−8) × (−3) = 24 and −18 ÷ 3 = −6.

Worked example

A hiker starts 15 m below a reference point, climbs 8 m on each of 3 stages and descends 4 m. Find the final elevation.

−15 + 3 × 8 − 4 = −15 + 24 − 4 = 5 m above the reference. Check using a calculator.

Now you try

A freezer starts at −2°C and cools by 3°C each hour for 4 hours. It then warms by 5°C. Find the final temperature and check digitally.

Show worked solution

−2 + 4 × (−3) + 5 = −2 − 12 + 5 = −9°C.
