Lesson 01

Centre and spread

Mean and median describe centre; range describes spread.

Worked example

Find mean and range of 2, 4, 6, 8.

Mean = 2 + 4 + 6 + 84
= 204
= 5
Range = 8 − 2 = 6

Now you try

Find range of 3, 5, 9, 12.

Show worked solution

Range = 12 − 3
= 9

Lesson 02

Quartiles

Order values; use medians of lower and upper halves, excluding the overall median when odd.

Worked example

Find quartiles of 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13.

Median = 7
Lower half: 1, 3, 5 → Q1 = 3
Upper half: 9, 11, 13 → Q3 = 11

Now you try

Find Q1 of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14.

Show worked solution

Lower half: 2, 4, 6
Q1 = 4

Lesson 03

Five-number summaries

Report minimum, Q1, median, Q3 and maximum in order.

Worked example

Find summary of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14.

Minimum = 2
Q1 = 4
Median = 8
Q3 = 12
Maximum = 14

Now you try

Find median of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7.

Show worked solution

Middle (fourth) value = 4

Lesson 04

Interquartile range

IQR measures the spread of the middle half and is less sensitive to extremes than range.

Worked example

For summary 2, 4, 8, 12, 14, find IQR and range.

IQR = Q3 − Q1
= 12 − 4
= 8
Range = 14 − 2 = 12

Now you try

Find IQR when Q1 = 5 and Q3 = 13.

Show worked solution

IQR = 13 − 5
= 8

Lesson 05

Constructing box plots

Use a common numerical scale; whiskers extend to minimum and maximum.

Worked example

Draw a box plot for summary 2, 4, 8, 12, 14.

Mark 2 and 14 as whisker ends.
Draw box from 4 to 12.
Draw median line at 8.

Now you try

Which value is the right edge of the box for summary 1, 3, 5, 9, 12?

Show worked solution

Right edge = Q3 = 9

Lesson 06

Reading box plots

Read quartiles and extremes directly from the scale.

Worked example

Read the IQR from the displayed box plot.

Q1 = 4, Q3 = 12
IQR = 12 − 4
= 8

Now you try

Read the range from the displayed plot.

Show worked solution

Maximum = 14, minimum = 2
Range = 14 − 2 = 12

Lesson 07

Parallel box plots

Compare medians and spreads on the same scale.

Worked example

Compare A: 2,4,8,12,14 and B: 4,6,10,12,14.

Median A = 8; B = 10
IQR A = 12 − 4 = 8
IQR B = 12 − 6 = 6
B has higher centre and smaller IQR.

Now you try

Which has smaller IQR?

Show worked solution

A: 8
B: 6
B

Lesson 08

Quartiles from dot plots

Read every dot as one observation, then order the values.

Worked example

Find Q1 and Q3 for the displayed dot plot.

Values: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14
Q1 = 4
Q3 = 12

Now you try

Find the IQR of this dot plot.

Show worked solution

IQR = 12 − 4
= 8

Lesson 09

Histograms and quartiles

Grouped histograms generally locate quartile classes, not exact quartile values. Unit-width bins for integer data can recover exact values.

Worked example

Seven integer observations occur once each at 2,4,6,8,10,12,14. Find median.

Total frequency = 7
Median position = 4th
Median = 8

Now you try

Which value is Q3 for these observations?

Show worked solution

Upper half: 10,12,14
Q3 = 12

Lesson 10

Skewness

A long right tail indicates positive skew; a long left tail indicates negative skew.

Worked example

Describe 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 4, 12.

Most values are low.
Tail extends towards larger values.
Positively skewed.

Now you try

Describe 1, 9, 10, 10, 11, 12, 12.

Show worked solution

Tail extends towards smaller values.
Negatively skewed.

Lesson 11

Standard deviation

Standard deviation measures spread about the mean. Use digital tools and distinguish population from sample SD.

Worked example

Use population SD for 2, 2, 6, 6.

Mean = 164 = 4
Spreadsheet: STDEV.P(2,2,6,6)
Population SD = 2

Now you try

Find population SD of 3,3,7,7 using a digital tool.

Show worked solution

STDEV.P(3,3,7,7)
= 2

Lesson 12

Statistical conclusions

Compare centre, spread and shape in context; avoid causal claims.

Worked example

A and B have median times 8 and 10 minutes and IQRs 8 and 6 minutes. Compare.

A has lower typical time (8 vs 10).
B has less spread in middle half (6 vs 8).

Now you try

Which group is more consistent by IQR?

Show worked solution

6 < 8
B
