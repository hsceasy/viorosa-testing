Lesson 01

Simple interest recap

Simple interest is calculated on the original principal only.

Worked example

Invest $1000 at 5% p.a. for 3 years.

I = 1000 × 0.05 × 3
= $150
A = $1150

Now you try

Find simple interest on $2000 at 4% for 2 years.

Show worked solution

2000 × 0.04 × 2 = $160

Lesson 02

Compounding by repetition

Each period earns interest on the previous balance.

Worked example

Invest $1000 at 10% for 3 years.

Year 1: 1000 + 100 = 1100
Year 2: 1100 + 110 = 1210
Year 3: 1210 + 121 = $1331

Now you try

Find the balance after two years on $500 at 10%.

Show worked solution

500 × 1.1 × 1.1 = $605

Lesson 03

Growth factors

Convert the percentage rate to a decimal and add one.

Worked example

Find the multiplier for 6% growth.

r = 0.06
1 + r = 1.06

Now you try

Find the multiplier for 8% growth.

Show worked solution

1 + 0.08 = 1.08

Lesson 04

Compound interest formula

A = P(1 + r)ⁿ, where r is the rate per period and n is the number of periods.

Worked example

Invest $2000 at 5% for 3 years.

A = 2000(1.05)³
= $2315.25

Now you try

Invest $1000 at 4% for 2 years.

Show worked solution

1000(1.04)² = $1081.60

Lesson 05

Interest earned

Subtract the original principal from the final balance.

Worked example

Find interest on $1000 at 10% for 3 years.

A = 1000(1.1)³ = 1331
I = 1331 − 1000 = $331

Now you try

Find interest on $500 at 10% for 2 years.

Show worked solution

605 − 500 = $105

Lesson 06

Compounding periods

For a nominal annual rate compounded monthly, divide the annual rate by 12 and multiply years by 12.

Worked example

Invest $1000 at 12% p.a. compounded monthly for 2 years.

r = 0.12 ÷ 12 = 0.01
n = 24
A = 1000(1.01)²⁴ ≈ $1269.73

Now you try

Find the monthly rate for 6% p.a.

Show worked solution

6% ÷ 12 = 0.5%

Lesson 07

Digital tools

Use repeated multiplication or a spreadsheet formula to model balances without rounding intermediate values.

Worked example

Enter 1000 in B2 and use =B2*1.05 in B3, then fill down.

B3 = 1050
B4 = 1102.50
B5 = 1157.625

Now you try

What formula calculates the next balance from B4 at 5%?

Show worked solution

=B4*1.05

Lesson 08

Simple versus compound

Simple growth adds a constant amount; compound growth multiplies by a constant factor.

Worked example

Compare $1000 at 10% for 3 years.

Simple: 1000 + 300 = 1300
Compound: 1000(1.1)³ = 1331
Difference = $31

Now you try

Compare the same investments after 2 years.

Show worked solution

1210 − 1200 = $10

Lesson 09

Depreciation factor

Reducing-balance depreciation multiplies the value by 1 − r each period.

Worked example

A $1000 asset loses 20% each year.

Multiplier = 1 − 0.20 = 0.80
After one year: $800

Now you try

Find the factor for 15% depreciation.

Show worked solution

1 − 0.15 = 0.85

Lesson 10

Depreciation formula

V = P(1 − r)ⁿ models the remaining value.

Worked example

A $10000 asset depreciates at 10% for 3 years.

V = 10000(0.9)³
= $7290

Now you try

Find the value of $2000 after 2 years at 20% depreciation.

Show worked solution

2000(0.8)² = $1280

Lesson 11

Loss in value

Subtract the remaining value from the initial value.

Worked example

A $10000 asset is worth $7290 after depreciation.

Loss = 10000 − 7290
= $2710

Now you try

Find the loss on $2000 depreciating to $1280.

Show worked solution

2000 − 1280 = $720

Lesson 12

Comparing financial models

Compare balances over equal periods using the appropriate growth or decay factor.

Worked example

Compare $5000 growing at 4% with an asset losing 4% over 2 years.

Investment: 5000(1.04)² = $5408
Asset: 5000(0.96)² = $4608
Difference = $800

Now you try

Find the difference after one year.

Show worked solution

5200 − 4800 = $400
