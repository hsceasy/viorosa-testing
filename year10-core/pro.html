Lesson 01

Sample spaces

A sample space lists all possible outcomes.

Worked example

List outcomes of two coin tosses.

HH, HT, TH, TT
Four equally likely outcomes for fair coins.

Now you try

How many outcomes for three coin tosses?

Show worked solution

2 × 2 × 2 = 8

Lesson 02

Independent events

Independent events do not change each other's probabilities.

Worked example

Find P(two heads) for two fair coins.

12 × 12 = 14

Now you try

Find P(two sixes) for two fair dice.

Show worked solution

16 × 16 = 136

Lesson 03

Dependent events

Without replacement, the first draw changes the probabilities of the second.

Worked example

A bag has 3 red and 2 blue counters. Find P(R then R).

35 × 24 = 310

Now you try

Find P(B then B).

Show worked solution

25 × 14 = 110

Lesson 04

Tree diagrams

Multiply along a branch and add probabilities of separate successful paths.

Worked example

Two fair coins: find P(exactly one head).

P(HT) + P(TH)
14 + 14 = 12

Now you try

Find P(no heads).

Show worked solution

P(TT) = 14

Lesson 05

With replacement

Replacing the first object restores the original probabilities.

Worked example

Bag: 3 red, 2 blue. Find P(R then B) with replacement.

35 × 25 = 625

Now you try

Find P(R then R).

Show worked solution

35 × 35 = 925

Lesson 06

Without replacement

Reduce the total and the count of the selected colour after each draw.

Worked example

Bag: 3 red, 2 blue. Find P(R then B) without replacement.

35 × 24 = 310

Now you try

Find P(B then R).

Show worked solution

25 × 34 = 310

Lesson 07

Array diagrams

A grid can list equally likely ordered pairs.

Worked example

Two fair dice: find P(sum = 3).

Successful pairs (1,2),(2,1)
236 = 118

Now you try

Find P(sum = 2).

Show worked solution

Only (1,1), so 136.

Lesson 08

At least one

Use the complement when it is easier to calculate.

Worked example

Find P(at least one six) in two fair dice rolls.

1 − 56 × 56
= 1136

Now you try

Find P(at least one head) in two fair tosses.

Show worked solution

1 − 14 = 34

Lesson 09

Exactly one

Add disjoint paths that satisfy the condition.

Worked example

Bag: 3 red, 2 blue, without replacement. Find P(one red).

P(RB) + P(BR)
310 + 310 = 35

Now you try

Find P(both the same colour).

Show worked solution

1 − 35 = 25

Lesson 10

Experimental probability

Divide the observed frequency by the number of trials.

Worked example

In 80 two-coin trials, HH occurred 18 times.

1880 = 0.225 = 22.5%

Now you try

HH occurs 24 times in 100 trials. Find experimental probability.

Show worked solution

24100 = 0.24

Lesson 11

Designing simulations

Use a random generator with equally likely values to model fair outcomes.

Worked example

Simulate two fair coins with two independent random integers 0 or 1.

Let 0 = tails, 1 = heads.
Record each pair and repeat 100 times.
Compare the HH proportion with 0.25.

Now you try

What theoretical proportion of HH is expected?

Show worked solution

12 × 12 = 0.25

Lesson 12

Testing a hypothesis

Compare observed and theoretical probabilities; random variation is expected.

Worked example

A student predicts 25 HH outcomes in 100 trials but observes 28.

Observed 0.28 versus theoretical 0.25.
Difference 0.03.
Repeat more trials to investigate.

Now you try

In 200 trials, how many HH outcomes are expected?

Show worked solution

200 × 0.25 = 50
